Departure — layout and responsiveness (proposed)

The departure chapter restates its state on four surfaces, shouts settled facts in ALL-CAPS mono, and buries the most useful content — the on-arrival facts and the checklist — under three identical cards. The decision picker's grid class doesn't exist, so it stacks at every width. This mock gives the page one hero with a countdown tile, a real 3-column picker, a boarding-pass card, ledger rows for both payments, and a single owner-chipped checklist.

A · What's wrong today
  • Four surfaces restate the same state: the journey-now band, the .psteps4 facts, each .psec__hint, and the rail's "What you owe" pkv — "ticket invoice due" is written four times.
  • The decision picker has no grid: portal-grid portal-grid--3 — the --3 rule doesn't exist in app.css, so the three .pick cards stack single-column at every width (~297–310).
  • Shouting mono: settled states render as ALL-CAPS mono sentences ("BOOKED WITH CENTURY NIT · X IS YOUR TRAVEL OFFICER."); assignedOpsUserName can be an email → an uppercased email.
  • Section 2 "Your papers" mixes two things: the service-fee milestone (a payment) and the released documents (files); the payment card repeats the band CTA and the Finish card repeats it again — three CTAs to one route.
  • Section 4 "Finish" is always rendered, mostly as a paragraph explaining why you can't finish yet — dead weight until canComplete.
  • Checklist: inline-styled <li>s with 22px tick buttons (below the 44px floor), owner split as two lists ("mine / theirs"), evidence rows link to the vault without preselecting the doc.
  • On-arrival facts (report-by, orientation, briefing, pickup, accommodation, emergency contact) are pkv rows buried under the checklist inside section 3 — the most concrete info on the page, hidden last.
  • Rail: countdown ink card says "Not booked" most of the time; "What you owe" duplicates sections 1–2; "After landing" is a paragraph. On mobile the rail — countdown included — drops below everything.
  • Flight details (FlightRows) are inline key/value pairs with no boarding-pass hierarchy — route and time are the important bits.
  • Bottom line: 6+ sharp-cards of nearly identical weight — no hierarchy between "act now" and "for reference".
